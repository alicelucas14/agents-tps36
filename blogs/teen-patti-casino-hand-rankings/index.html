The Exact Order of Teen Patti Casino Hand Rankings (Highest to Lowest)

Here is the standard hierarchy used in most Indian home games and the majority of online Teen Patti tables:

  1. Trail (Trio / Three of a Kind)
  2. Pure Sequence (Straight Flush / Pure Run)
  3. Sequence (Straight / Run)
  4. Colour (Flush / Color)
  5. Pair
  6. High Card

A weak Trail still beats the strongest Pure Sequence. A low Sequence beats any Colour. This order is fixed by tradition and is what you will see on almost every reputable Indian online platform.

Detailed Breakdown of Each Hand

  1. Trail (Highest Hand)

Three cards of the same rank.

Example: A♠ A♥ A♦ or 7♣ 7♦ 7♥

Three Aces is the absolute best. Three Twos is the lowest Trail, but it still wins against everything below it.

Approximate chance: 0.24% (about 1 in 425 hands).

  1. Pure Sequence

Three consecutive cards of the same suit.

Example: 5♥ 6♥ 7♥ or A♠ 2♠ 3♠

In traditional Indian rules (still followed by many online tables), A-2-3 is the highest Pure Sequence, followed by A-K-Q, then K-Q-J, down to 4-3-2.

Note: K-A-2 is almost never accepted as a valid run.

Chance: roughly 0.22%.

  1. Sequence (Normal Run)

Three consecutive cards of mixed suits.

Example: 9♠ 10♥ J♦

Same ranking order as Pure Sequence — A-2-3 is usually strongest, then A-K-Q, and so on. A Pure Sequence always beats a normal Sequence of the same ranks.

Chance: about 3.26%.

  1. Colour (Flush)

Three cards of the same suit that are not consecutive.

Example: K♦ 9♦ 4♦

When two players both have Colour, compare the highest card first, then the second, then the third. A-K-J is the strongest possible Colour; 5-3-2 is the weakest.

Chance: around 4.96%.

  1. Pair

Two cards of the same rank plus one kicker.

Example: Q♠ Q♥ 8♣

Higher pair wins. If the pairs are equal, the kicker decides. Pair of Aces with a King is the best; pair of Twos with a Three is the worst.

Chance: roughly 16.94%.

  1. High Card

No pair, no sequence, no flush.

The highest card decides, then the second, then the third. Ace-King-Jack is the strongest High Card hand.

This is what you get most of the time — about 74% of all deals.

Important Rules That Catch New Players in India

  • A-2-3 is frequently treated as the top sequence in classic Indian play. Some modern online casinos rank A-K-Q highest instead. Always check the table rules before you sit.
  • Sequence beats Colour. This is the opposite of regular five-card poker and is one of the biggest traps for players coming from Texas Hold’em.
  • Suits are usually equal. Only a few house rules rank Spades > Hearts > Diamonds > Clubs for absolute ties.
  • Blind vs Seen play changes the bet size but never changes the hand rankings.

Why These Rankings Matter for Indian Players

Teen Patti is played everywhere — family gatherings during festivals, local clubs, and now thousands of online tables accessible via UPI. Knowing the exact order lets you:

  • Fold weak High Cards early instead of chasing
  • Raise confidently with a Trail or Pure Sequence
  • Understand when a Sequence is strong enough to call a big bet
  • Spot when opponents are overvaluing a Colour

The math is simple once you internalise the hierarchy. Most hands you will see are High Card or Pair. The really strong hands (Trail and Pure Sequence) appear less than once every 200 hands combined. That scarcity is why they command respect at the table.
