Why the Teen Patti Sequence List Matters in India

Teen Patti (also called 3 Patti) is deeply rooted in Indian culture. It’s fast, thrilling, and perfect for those friendly (or intense!) betting rounds. The rules are simple: three cards per player, and the best hand wins. But the real skill comes from knowing the Teen Patti sequence list—the hierarchy that decides every pot.

Pro tip: Always confirm house rules before starting, as some local variations treat A-2-3 differently, but we’ll cover the most common Indian standards here.

Complete Teen Patti Hand Rankings (Sequence List from Highest to Lowest)

Here’s the standard Teen Patti sequence list used in most games across India:

  1. Trail (Trio or Three of a Kind) – The Ultimate Boss Hand Three cards of the exact same rank. This crushes almost everything. Examples:
    • A-A-A (strongest possible)
    • K-K-K
    • 5-5-5 (still beats any sequence or color)
    • 2-2-2 (weakest trail) Real-life vibe: Getting three Aces feels like hitting a jackpot during a festive Diwali game!
  2. Pure Sequence (Straight Flush or Pakki Sequence) – Super Strong & Rare Three consecutive cards of the same suit. Pure beauty! Common Examples (ranked high to low):
    • A-K-Q of Hearts (or any suit)
    • A-2-3 of Spades (Ace plays low here in most rules)
    • K-Q-J of Diamonds
    • Q-J-10 of Clubs
    • 10-9-8, 9-8-7, down to 4-3-2 of the same suit Pure Sequence always beats a normal Sequence of the same cards.
  3. Sequence (Normal Run or Straight) – Solid but Not Pure Three consecutive cards, but mixed suits. Examples:
    • A-K-Q (mixed suits)
    • A-2-3 (mixed)
    • J-10-9 (mixed)
    • 7-6-5 (mixed)
    • Down to 4-3-2 Note: K-A-2 or 2-A-K usually doesn’t count as a sequence— no wrap-around in standard rules.
  4. Color (Flush) – Same Suit, No Sequence Needed Any three cards of the same suit, not in a row. Examples:
    • A-K-J of Spades
    • K-9-4 of Hearts
    • 8-6-2 of Clubs (still a color!) In ties, compare the highest card first, then the next, and so on.
  5. Pair (Double) – Two of a Kind Exactly two cards of the same rank + one unrelated card. Examples:
    • A-A-Q (strong)
    • Q-Q-7
    • 3-3-2 (weakest) The pair decides first; the kicker card breaks ties.
  6. High Card – The Default When Nothing Else Hits No pairs, no sequences, no colors. Highest card wins. Examples:
    • A-K-J (mixed suits, strongest high card)
    • K-Q-10
    • Down to something like 5-4-3 (weak) Most hands (around 74%) end up here, so knowing your high cards matters a lot!

Tie-Breakers: How to Settle Close Calls

  • Same hand type? Compare the highest card first.
  • For Trails: Higher rank wins (A-A-A > K-K-K).
  • For Sequences/Pure: Highest top card (A-K-Q > K-Q-J).
  • For Color or High Card: Highest → second → third card.
  • Suits usually don’t matter unless the table has a specific rule (Spades > Hearts etc., rare in casual play).

Pro Tips for Indian Players Mastering the Teen Patti Sequence List

  • Blind vs Seen: Playing blind (without looking) is thrilling but risky—great when you’re on a hot streak during those long weekend sessions.
  • Bluff Smartly: In India, where we love the drama, a confident chaal (bet) with a strong high card can force folds from weaker sequences.
  • Practice on Apps: Many popular Indian platforms let you play free or low-stakes to memorize these rankings without pressure.
  • Bankroll Wisdom: Start small, especially in local games—Teen Patti is for fun and bonding, not emptying your pocket!

Remember, even the best Teen Patti sequence list knowledge won’t win every hand. Luck, reading opponents, and timing your bets are just as important.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

What is the strongest hand in the Teen Patti sequence list?

Trail (Three Aces) is unbeatable.

Does A-2-3 count as a high sequence?

Yes, in most Indian Teen Patti games, A-2-3 is one of the strongest sequences (often second after A-K-Q for pure/normal).

Is Pure Sequence better than normal Sequence?

Absolutely—same cards but all one suit wins.

Can I play Teen Patti online legally in India?

Many states allow skill-based games. Stick to reputable apps and check local laws.
