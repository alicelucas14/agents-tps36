1. The Boot (Ante) – Everyone Pays to Start

Before any cards appear, every player puts the same fixed amount into the pot. This is called the boot or ante.

Think of it as the entry ticket. If the table boot is ₹10, everyone contributes ₹10. That money forms the starting pot. No one can sit out this step.

2. The Deal – Three Cards Face Down

The dealer (or the software in online casinos) gives each player three cards face down. You do not look at them yet. The cards stay hidden until you decide whether to play blind or seen.

3. Blind or Seen – Your First Big Decision

This choice shapes the entire betting round.

  • Blind player: You keep your cards face down. You bet without knowing your hand. This keeps the pressure on others and lets you bet smaller amounts.
  • Seen player: You look at your cards. Now you know your strength, but you must bet more (usually double) to stay in.

You can start blind and switch to seen later on your turn. Once you look, you stay seen for the rest of that hand. You cannot go back to blind.

4. The Main Betting Round – Chaal, Call, Raise or Pack

Betting starts with the player to the left of the dealer and moves clockwise. There is no “check” option. On your turn you must either put money in or leave.

Example

Boot is ₹10. First player goes blind and puts ₹10. Current stake is now ₹10.

Next player is seen → must put at least ₹20. If they put ₹40, the next player’s current stake drops to ₹20.

On every turn you can:

  • Pack (Fold): Throw your cards away and lose whatever you’ve already put in. You’re out until the next hand.
  • Chaal / Call: Match the required amount and stay in.
  • Raise: Increase the bet within the allowed limit. This forces everyone still in to match the new amount or pack.

The round keeps going around the table. Pots grow fast when players keep raising.

5. Side Show – The Secret Comparison

When three or more players remain, a seen player can ask for a side show with the player who acted just before them (only if that player is also seen).

Both players quietly compare cards. The one with the weaker hand must pack. The stronger hand stays in without revealing anything to the rest of the table. Blind players cannot ask for or accept a side show.

6. The Final Show – When Only Two Players Are Left

Once only two players remain, either one can call for a show.

  • If both are seen, the cost is usually twice the current stake.
  • If one is still blind, the rules may differ slightly by platform, but the blind player often pays only the current stake.

Both hands are revealed. Highest ranking hand wins the entire pot. If the hands are identical, the player who did not call the show usually takes the pot (house rules can vary, so check the table rules).

If everyone else packs before a show is called, the last player standing wins the pot without showing cards.

Quick Recap of a Full Teen Patti Casino Betting Round

  1. Everyone pays the boot.
  2. Three cards are dealt face down.
  3. Players choose blind or seen.
  4. Betting continues clockwise with chaal, raise or pack.
  5. Side shows can eliminate weaker hands privately.
  6. When two players remain, a show decides the winner (or the last player standing takes it all).
